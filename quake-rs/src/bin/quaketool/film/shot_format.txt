# The world: a map, or a recorded demo.
map e1m1                       the map (maps/e1m1.bsp), its game running live
map gen:grazing                or a map made for the film, generated as the shot starts (mapgen.rs:
                               `grazing`, a long hall of id's base for the perspective span)
demo demo1 [from S]            or a recorded demo, from S seconds in (default 0)
skill N                        0..3 (default 1)
warmup S | until T             game seconds run (undrawn) before the first frame (default 1),
                               or until the game's clock (cl.time) reaches T
wake X,Y,Z [at T]              wake the monster nearest the point (its enemy the player, QuakeC's
                               FoundTarget) at film second T (default 0); a line each

# What the player does, at film seconds (each line `[at T]`, default 0; in line order at one time).
impulse N [at T]               the console's `impulse N` (9: every weapon, 1..8: choose one)
attack on|off [at T]           `+attack` / `-attack`
fire NAME [at T]               every entity whose targetname is NAME used, as a trigger's
                               SUB_UseTargets uses it, the player its activator (e1m7's `t4`: Chthon)
look P,Y [at T]                the player's view angles (pitch + down, yaw), for `camera player`
cmd COMMAND [ARGS] [at T]      a console command: +/-attack, +/-jump, +/-forward, +/-back,
                               +/-moveleft, +/-moveright, +/-movedown, or god, noclip, fly, kill,
                               impulse N, give ITEM [N]

# The film.
duration S                     seconds of film (required)
fps N                          frames a second of film (default 60)
size WxH                       the output frames (default 1920x1080)
frames A..B                    only film frames A to B-1 written (the game still runs from frame 0,
                               undrawn before A): a window of a longer shot, its clock unchanged
threads N                      the renderer's threads (default every core); the pixels are the same
                               on any number, but for `xray bands`, which shows them

# The game's settings.
preset slop|classic            every setting at once (default slop)
cvar NAME VALUE [at T]         any console variable, after the preset (r_perspspan 16, gamma 0.8, ...);
                               with `at T`, from film second T on (a span flipping 64, 32, 16, 8, a
                               torch's flicker stepping up); the sound's mixer stays the shot's first
                               (`snd_modern` cannot change mid-shot; `volume` can). The film reads
                               its own lines, not these: `host_maxfps` (`clock`, `stepping`),
                               `vid_pixelsize` (`pixel`), `r_threads` (`threads`), and
                               `sv_max_edicts` is the map's from its start
mode WxH                       the picture the game draws (default: slop the size over `pixel`,
                               Classic the preset's video mode, 960x600)
pixel N                        slop's pixel size: mode = size / N, each pixel N x N (default 1)
display W:H|square             the shape the picture is shown at (default: slop square, Classic 4:3)
display HZ                     the screen the film watches (10 to 1000 Hz): on each of its refreshes
                               the game's own gate decides, as the page's does, whether a host frame
                               runs (`clock id`: id's 72 fps cap passes every refresh at 60 Hz, every
                               4th at 240; `clock free`: every refresh), and a film frame shows the
                               last picture drawn. The screen's seconds are the game's: `display 240`
                               with `speed 0.25` at fps 60 is a 240 Hz screen at quarter speed, a
                               refresh a film frame. One line can say both: `display 4:3 240`.
                               Default: no screen, the film is one (see `clock`)
hud on|off [at T]              the status bar at the `viewsize` cvar's Screen size (the preset's, or a
                               `cvar viewsize` line's), or none: Screen size 120, whatever `viewsize`
                               says (default off)
gun on|off [at T]              the first-person weapon (default off)
crosshair 0|1|2|on|off [at T]  the `crosshair` cvar, as a `cvar crosshair` line (default 0, the slop
                               preset's cross too)
messages on|off [at T]         centre prints and notify lines (default off)
                               (These five, `cvar` and `body` take `at T`: from film second T on.)
notarget on|off                monsters do not see the player (default on)
player spawn|camera|X,Y,Z[,YAW]  where the player is: the map's start, carried along with the
                               camera (noclip; the map's exits are shut), or stood there
                               (default spawn)
clock id|free                  the game's frame gate: id's (`Host_FilterTime`: at most 72 host frames
                               a second) or the uncapped page's (a host frame every refresh, stepped
                               as the uncapped page steps) (default: Classic id, slop free). With
                               `display`, it runs on the screen's refreshes. Without, the film is the
                               screen: `free` is a host frame every film frame, `id` id's ticks, a
                               host frame every 1/72 s of game time, each picture held to the film
                               frames after it (slow motion shows the ticks; at 60 fps one tick in six
                               never shows, a judder no 60 Hz screen has: `display 60` shows what one
                               does)
speed S [at T]                 the game's speed from film second T on (default 1 from 0; 0.25 is slow
                               motion; 0 freezes the world while the camera goes on); with `clock
                               free` and no `display` the game runs at fps / S host frames a second:
                               fps 60 and speed 0.25 is the game at 240 Hz
stepping id|uncapped           how a host frame steps the game, if not as its clock does (id: id's
                               per-frame code, which drifts away from 72 Hz; uncapped: slop's, which
                               lands on id's 72 Hz results at any rate): `clock free`, `speed 0.125`
                               and `stepping id` is id's code at 480 Hz
bob on|off                     a path camera bobs as the game bobs a running player's eye (V_CalcBob,
                               from the path's own speed on the game's clock); default off
body on|off [at T]             the player's own model drawn (default off: id's client never draws
                               it), for a camera that looks at the player (a demo's recorded one too)

# The camera. Angles in degrees, as Quake's (quaketool view --angles): pitch + looks down,
# yaw 0 along +x and 90 along +y; X,Y,Z is the eye.
camera player                  the player's own eye (the default for a map)
camera demo                    the recording's camera, with the client's lerp (the default for a demo)
camera fixed X,Y,Z P,Y[,R]     still
camera fixed X,Y,Z at X,Y,Z    still, looking at a point
camera path                    keyframes (below): positions through Catmull-Rom (a cubic Hermite with
                               each key's tangent from its neighbours over their times), angles by the
                               shortest arc, each segment eased as its first key says
key T X,Y,Z P,Y[,R] [fov F] [ease linear|in|out|inout]
key T X,Y,Z at X,Y,Z [fov F] [ease ...]      looking at a point (the point is interpolated too)
camera walk [run|walk|SPEED] [within R]
                               the player walks the keys' route as a player does, and the camera is its
                               own eye (walk.rs): each host frame the film sends what a player at the keys
                               and mouse sends (the view's angles, the forward and side speeds, the jump
                               button) and the game's own movement moves it, so the steps, slopes, walls,
                               the bob and the stair smoothing are the game's (the gun too, `gun on`). It
                               starts standing at the first key and walks at most SPEED units a second
                               (run: 320, sv_maxspeed, the default; walk: 200), reaching each key within R
                               units across (default 16) and 40 up or down. A stuck player (no nearer the
                               next key for a game second) is an error, before a frame is drawn. The report
                               gives the film second each key was reached at against its T, and the walk's
                               length against the shot's; walk.csv has a row a picture: the player's origin,
                               the eye drawn (bob and stair smoothing in it), the view, the speed. The walk
                               is planned once and its moves replayed by game second, so both sides of an
                               `ab` send the same commands (the report says how near each side's player
                               kept). The `player` line does not apply, nor `bob` (the eye bobs as the
                               game's does); `look` and `cmd +forward` lines are refused
key T X,Y,Z [P,Y | at X,Y,Z | look P,Y] [jump] [ease E]
                               (after `camera walk`) a waypoint: the eye's place (the player's origin + 22)
                               to reach at film second T, at the pace that gets there then (a stand, if it
                               would be slower than 50); the view turns from the look of the key before to
                               this one's over the way, as far as the player has come (a key with none
                               looks along the route ahead); `jump`: the player jumps as it leaves the key;
                               a key at the place of the one before is a pause until its T
fov F                          the field of view of the film's cameras (90; Hor+ widens it on a wide
                               picture); `camera player` and `camera demo` see id's 90, and refuse it

# Cameras that follow the game. ENTITY is `player` (a demo's: its recorded player, at the eye), an
# entity's number (an edict; `events.json`'s pose events name them), or CLASS[#N]: the Nth (from 0) of
# a class to appear in the shot (those there at its start in entity order, then each as it appears: a
# nail fired later is a later one), CLASS a classname (monster_army, spike), a model or its short
# name (soldier; a demo knows id1's classnames by their models). e1m1's first grunt is monster_army#1
# (at skill 1: a skill's monsters are its own).
# Before it renders, the shot's game is run once, undrawn (a rehearsal), to learn where the
# entity goes: the camera knows the future. It follows the entity's place as the game has it (the
# middle of its box; a demo's: its origin), not the model as drawn, which is the preset's business
# (Classic steps it, slop glides it): so an `ab` of the two has one camera. That place is smoothed
# over a box as long as a monster's step (0.1 s) and a Gaussian of half that: id's 10 Hz steps
# become a motion whose speed changes smoothly. Lags and lookaheads are in game seconds.
camera follow ENTITY [offset X,Y,Z] [lag S] [lookahead S]
                               the eye rides with the entity at the offset (world axes; default 0),
                               looking at it: `lag`, the eye trails it by S seconds, eased in and out
                               (a critically damped spring); `lookahead`, it aims at where the entity
                               will be S seconds on. `key` lines after it are a path about the
                               entity: each key's X,Y,Z the eye's offset, `at X,Y,Z` a point from the
                               one aimed at (`at 0,0,0`: the entity), angles as given
camera orbit X,Y,Z|ENTITY [radius R] [height H] [speed DEG/S] [from YAW] [lag S]
                               the eye circles the point or the entity at R (default 128), H above it,
                               DEG/S (default 20, + anticlockwise from above) from bearing YAW at film
                               second 0, looking at it
aim ENTITY [offset X,Y,Z] [lag S] [lookahead S]
                               a path, fixed or orbit camera looks at the entity (plus the offset),
                               its keys' angles ignored: a fixed camera pans, a dolly keeps it in frame
                               A camera fastened to an entity or an orbit keeps out of the walls: where
                               the world comes between it and what it follows it is pulled in, 8 units
                               short of the wall, easing in before and out after. `player camera`
                               cannot ride a camera that follows the game (the player would move it).

# Marks: points followed onto the picture, for an edit's callouts (marks.rs).
mark NAME X,Y,Z [radius R]     a point of the world
mark NAME entity ENTITY [offset X,Y,Z] [radius R]
                               an entity's place as this frame draws it (a gliding monster where it
                               glides), plus the offset
                               Each frame, events.json's `marks` gets the mark's place on the output
                               frame (`screen`, in front of the camera, also off the picture), whether
                               it is `in_view` and `visible` (in the view, and nothing the frame drew
                               there, by its z-buffer, is nearer than the point less R: default 8 for
                               a point, 16 for an entity's middle) and its `dist`ance from the eye
markdraw NAME [on|off]         draw the mark's tag on the frames (after its `mark` line), for previews:
                               corner ticks and its name, flame when seen, slate when hidden

# Colours on these lines are RRGGBB (hex, no `#`, which starts a comment) or pN, Quake's palette entry N.
# X-ray: the renderer's machinery, drawn from what it decided for the 3-D view.
xray MODE                      the picture: game (default), black, z (the z-buffer, near light),
                               surfaces (a colour each, id's r_drawflat), spans (each surface's
                               spans, a row each), segments (each span's affine runs in rust and
                               brown, a lava tick at each perspective divide), mip (the mip level:
                               0 green, 1 yellow, 2 orange, 3 red), cache (each surface-cache block
                               a tile, darker for coarser mips, black borders), bakes (the blocks
                               this frame baked, lit), luxels (the lightmap's 16-texel cells),
                               leaves (the BSP leaf of each visible point), lightmaps (the light
                               alone), error (the affine texel's distance from the exact one: dark
                               none, yellow half a texel, red one or more), pixelsoff [COLOUR]
                               [alpha A] [dim D] (the pixels the perspective span puts off the exact
                               texel, tinted ff3d8b over the picture; the rest dimmed by D, default 0),
                               bands (each renderer thread's bands of rows, a colour a thread: which
                               thread takes which band is the scheduler's, the pixels the same)
divides on|off [COLOUR] [width W] [alpha A]
                               a thin mark at each perspective divide (where the walls' and liquids'
                               texel is found exactly: every 16 pixels in id's spans, 8 in slop's,
                               every pixel at exact) over the picture: colour 39c2ff, W output pixels
                               wide (default a 720th of the frame's height), alpha 0.85
wire world|entities|all|off [hidden|through] [pvs|culled]
                               polygon edges over the picture: the world's, the entities' (brush
                               models, alias models' triangles); hidden by what is in front, or
                               through it; the world's coloured drawn / in the PVS / culled, or
                               only the culled ones
vis X,Y,Z                      draw the PVS of this point instead of the camera's (r_lockpvs)
mix T VALUE                    the x-ray's strength over the game's picture at film second T
                               (keys interpolated; default 1); on `xray lightmaps`, between 0 and 1,
                               each frame is drawn twice, the game's and the light alone (redrawn
                               paused), and cross-faded

# On and off in one pass: the shot rendered twice, A and B, stepped side by side.
ab LINES | LINES               each side's own lines, after the shot's (`;` between a side's lines):
                               `ab cvar r_torchflicker 0 | cvar r_torchflicker 1`
ablabels TEXT | TEXT           each side's label (conchars, bronze)
split line|side|stack|diff     one frame cut by a vertical line (A left, B right; default), the
                               halves side by side or stacked (each framed for its half), or A with
                               each pixel B draws otherwise tinted lava orange
splitat T X                    the line's place at film second T, 0 the left edge to 1 the right
                               (keys interpolated, default 0.5): `splitat 1 1` + `splitat 3 0` wipes
                               B in from the right over two seconds
absound a|b|both               whose sound: A's, B's (default), or B's in sound.wav and each side's
                               in sound-a.wav and sound-b.wav

# Output.
label TEXT                     burned into every frame in Quake's lettering (conchars, bronze)
labelpos tl|tr|bl|br           its corner (default bl)
sound on|game|off              write sound.wav, the shot's game sound (default off): `on`, each sound
                               starts with the frame that shows its cause and plays at its own speed,
                               in slow motion too; `game`, the mixer on the game's clock, stretched to
                               the film's (slow motion slows and lowers it; a frozen world is silent)
events on|off                  write events.json: each sound started, muzzle flash and monster pose
                               change on screen, with its film second, frame and place (events.rs);
                               a shot with marks writes it too, with them
